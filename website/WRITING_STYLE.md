# Writing style for the CleanSlate essay

Distilled from Sophie L. Wang's research pages and blog (sophielwang.com: /cues, /sensory, 12 posts).
Use the voice, not the words.

## Voice
- "We" in research prose; "you" only to tell the reader what to do with a figure.
- Warm, plain, curious. 12–25 word sentences, an occasional short punchy one. Question → answer.
- Paragraphs of 2–5 sentences, one job each: set up, claim, number, point to the figure.

## Opening
- Tl;dr of three sentences: the headline result, the mechanism, the surprising twist.
- Then one paragraph saying what this page is for, and where details live (the paper).
- Background: prior answers, then pivot: "In this paper, we ask…".

## Around figures
- The paragraph before a figure: italic finding ("We find that…"), one number with a comparison point,
  a twist, then a pointer sentence: "Below, you can explore…".
- After a figure: a short "In fact," paragraph with a second, finer observation.
- Say how to read it in operational terms: what each mark, colour, outline and control is.
- Captions: short and technical (what the panel shows, how numbers are computed).

## Claims and terms
- Findings in italics as full sentences; numbers always with a comparison.
- Hedge in the discussion, never in the results.
- Bold a new term once and gloss it ("forced opening tokens, i.e., **token cues**"); keep the name.
- One vivid running example, returned to throughout (here: *Never Gonna Give You Up*).

## Headings, asides, ending
- Section headings: short claims ("A song is not in one place").
- Asides in parentheses; jokes in parentheses or footnotes.
- End with Implications: 2–3 short subsections ending in open questions, then a quote, references, BibTeX.
  No summary "Conclusion", no marketing tone.
